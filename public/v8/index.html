        How it works

        You bring the model. We provide the robots, operators, scenes and scoring.

        1. ApplyTell us about your policy and the robots you want results on, in a short form.
        2. Connect your modelWe help you link your inference server through our policy SDK, or serve your checkpoint on a machine next to the robot.
        3. We evaluate it in the wildOperators set up each scene and run your model in blind comparisons and repeated task trials, recording every camera.
        4. Results go publicScores land on the leaderboard with their uncertainty, linked to the videos and data behind them.

        FAQ

        Who can apply?

        Teams with a policy for one of our robots. The 10,000 free hours are allocated by the RoboColosseum team, who review every application and follow up with next steps.

        What happens after I apply?

        We read your application and get in touch to plan the evaluation with you: which robots and tasks, and how your model will connect. Then the runs start and results appear as they are scored.

        Which robots are available?

        Unitree G1 humanoids, bimanual YAM stations, Franka arms in the DROID setup and SO-ARM101 arms. Earth Rover Mini+ wheeled robots are being added. Your policy has to take the robot's camera inputs and produce its action format.

        How do I connect my model?

        Two ways. Run your own inference server and connect it with our policy SDK, and the robot streams observations to it during each run. Or hand over a checkpoint and we serve it on a machine next to the robot. We help you set up either one.

        How is a model scored?

        Head-to-head: two unnamed models try the same fresh instruction and the evaluator picks the better run or calls a tie. Ratings are fitted from all comparisons on that robot and shown with their uncertainty. Standard tasks: every model is fine-tuned on the same 100 demonstrations, then makes 25 attempts, and we report full successes and milestone progress.

        How is it kept fair?

        Evaluators see run A and run B, never the model names. Both models in a comparison face the same robot, scene and instruction, one after the other. Every fine-tuned model trains on identical demonstrations.

        Can I compare a score on one robot with another?

        No. Each robot type keeps its own board, because a rating earned on a Franka arm tells you nothing about a humanoid.

        Why real robots instead of simulation?

        Simulated benchmarks saturate: policies above 90% on LIBERO can fall close to zero once the scene is perturbed (LIBERO-PRO). Physical scenes vary on their own and expose failures a simulator does not model.

        What gets published, and where?

        Every run from both kinds of evaluation, failures included, with its videos, in LeRobot format on Hugging Face. Scores go on the public leaderboard.
