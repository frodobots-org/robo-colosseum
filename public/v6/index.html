      Head-to-head

      Can it handle a task it has never seen?

      An evaluator writes a new instruction on the spot. Two anonymous models attempt it back to back on the same robot, in the same scene, and the evaluator judges which run was better, or a tie. Ratings are fitted from every comparison on that robot.

      Standard tasks

      How well does it learn a new task?

      Every model is fine-tuned on the same 100 demonstrations of a defined task, then attempts it 25 times. We report full success and how far each attempt got against written milestones.

      What keeps the scores honest

      • Evaluators don't know which model they are judging.
      • Both models in a matchup get the same robot, scene and instruction, one after the other.
      • Every fine-tuned model learns from the same demonstrations.
      • Scores are never pooled across robots, and every run is published.

        Questions

        Who can submit a policy?

        Any team with a policy for one of our robots. Academic labs evaluate for free, from a pool of 10,000 hours.

        Which robots can I evaluate on?

        Unitree G1 humanoids, bimanual YAM arms, Franka arms in the DROID setup and SO-ARM101 arms today. Earth Rover Mini+ wheeled robots are joining. Your policy needs to accept the robot's cameras and produce its action format.

        How do I submit?

        Either connect your inference server through our policy SDK, so the robot streams observations to it during each run, or send a checkpoint and we serve it next to the robot.

        How are models scored?

        Head-to-head: two anonymous models attempt the same new instruction and an evaluator picks the better run or a tie; ratings are fitted from every comparison on that robot, with uncertainty. Standard tasks: every model is fine-tuned on the same 100 demonstrations and attempts the task 25 times; we report full success and progress against written milestones.

        Do evaluators know which model they are judging?

        No. In a head-to-head the two models appear as A and B, and the evaluator judges the runs without knowing which model produced them.

        Can I compare a score on one robot with another?

        No. Each robot has its own leaderboard. A rating on a Franka arm says nothing about a humanoid, so we never pool them.

        What gets published?

        Every rollout from both measurements, including failures, with its videos, in LeRobot format on Hugging Face, plus the scores on the public leaderboard.
