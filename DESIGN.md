# RoboColosseum design language

The decisions behind the landing pages, as the user stated them. Read this before touching any page under `public/`. When a decision changes, change it here first.

## Launch meeting and the new brand (27 Oct 2026, BIT-3373): `/v8/`

The 26 Oct launch meeting (launch on 4 Nov: X thread, website and launch video together) and Ben's brand in Figma (file `VIYsAXvWfmt8My4PfpuumK`, page "RC Brand") replace several rules below. Where this section and an older bullet disagree, this section wins.

- **Hero names the activity:** it must say evaluation **and** benchmark (user, 27 Oct: "It should have both evaluation AND benchmark"); "Physical AI, in the wild" alone read as robot deployment services. Headline: *Physical AI evaluation and benchmarking, in the wild.*
- **One call to action: "Apply for free evaluation"** (an expression of interest; "Submit your policy" asked too much, since onboarding takes real engineering work). Every copy goes to the application form embedded at the foot of the landing page (`#apply`), never to a separate page. The form is a UForm that Karen brands and Santiago builds; until it exists the slot says the form opens there soon. Follow-up: Ian contacts applicants; strong university fits go to Jiafei.
- **The 10,000 hours are free evaluation** (the open question below is settled): a large band directly under the hero, the page's one loud moment. The page does not state who is eligible beyond "teams with a policy for one of our robots"; allocation is the team's call.
- **Order:** hero (claim, film, founders' logos) → 10,000-hour offer → the leaderboard → in the wild → our robots → our scale → how it works → evaluations (a preview of the evaluation viewer: both runs in sync, camera choice) → open data (the Hugging Face repositories themselves) → FAQ → the application form.
- **Numbers are counted once a day, not live**, and carry the date: "Updated 27 Oct 2026" in the hero status line, the leaderboard and the scale band. They come from `/api/stats` and the published boards, written by the daily `robocolosseum-leaderboard` job (colosseum-router PR #8). If `/api/stats` is unavailable the page counts the same figures from the evaluation list in the browser (slower, fallback only).
- **Scale wording:** "hundreds of robots" (the meeting's notes said "hundreds of embodiments"; we have five robot types, so robots it is). No fleet counts.
- **Open data:** list the actual repositories (training sets per robot with episode counts, the evaluation sets) and link the organisation. The Earth Rover drives stay off the page until their release, licence and GPS precision are decided.
- **Brand** (see Logo, Colour and Type below): light theme by default, dark theme on a switch in the top bar (remembered per device).
- **Second round of feedback (user, 27 Oct 2026):**
  - No status pills or stat lines in the hero ("those top notes 15k runs... I don't like them").
  - **The hero is the film, full bleed, behind the claim** ("I loved before was like in the bg... the hero space is very important"). The film is cut from the team's own footage (Drive folder of Franka, YAM and SO-101 camera files, the humanoid team's clips and the Earth Rover's front camera), bright, with people around, 12 shots of about 2.4 s, ending on the room full of G1 humanoids; `public/v8/video/hero.mp4` + `hero.json` (captions: robot, place, date). It stays dark in both themes, with a shade from the lower left.
  - **The 10,000 hours are a callout, not a band**, "like websites use for cookies": a dark notice with the one button, bottom right (a bottom sheet on phones). It appears once the reader scrolls, steps aside while the form is on screen, and stays closed for the visit once dismissed.
  - **In the wild shows scale growing:** a film that starts on one run and pulls back four times into 9, 81, 729 and 6,561 runs (`mosaic.mp4`, made by `tools/mosaic.py` in the session scratchpad: the two outer rings are 6,432 distinct real evaluation recordings from the API plus rover drives, no run shown twice), with a counter ending on "6,561 recordings on screen, of N recorded runs"; the dark YAM tile of the old wall is gone. Beside it, the **Earth Rover's own camera** among passers-by in Wuhan (`rover.mp4`, from the drives with people around: rides 155717, 155791, 155703, 155761, 155807).

- **Third round of feedback (user, 27 Oct 2026):**
  - **Hero:** *Benchmarking Physical AI / Eval In the Wild*, on two lines, over the film.
  - **Every face in every video is blurred** ("BLUR all people's faces. This is a general direction for ALL videos from now on"). People are found with a pose model (YOLO11m-pose: it catches profiles and small people that face detectors miss) at full resolution before any downscaling, and a padded head region is blurred and held for a few frames; humanoid-only clips are left alone (the model takes humanoids for people). Tool: `tools/blurfaces.py` in the session scratchpad. Live recordings streamed by the evaluation viewer are not processed.
  - **Section names:** the evaluations film is "Real-world evaluations" (not "In the wild", which the hero already says); "Multiple embodiments" for the robots; "Evaluation viewer" for the comparison preview. The top bar says Leaderboard, Evaluations, Embodiments, How it works, Open data, FAQ.
  - **The rover's solo reel is gone** ("unnecessary"); rovers stay in the hero film and the evaluations film.
  - **Our scale** is not six numbers in a grid any more: one large total (recorded runs) with a bar splitting it into head-to-head runs and standard-task trials, then four cards that show breadth: scenes as one square per scene by robot, the evaluators' own instructions scrolling, the logos of the labs whose policies were evaluated, and the countries and robot types.
  - **Buttons, not small links:** "Full leaderboard" and "Open the evaluation viewer" are outlined buttons with an arrow. Open data carries **"Get 10,000 hours of free evals"** with the Hugging Face logo, an orange button that, like every call to action, goes to the application form (a Youform; Karen builds it, its link goes into `FORM_URL` in `public/v8/chrome.js` and it is embedded in the closing section).
  - **The closing section is the offer, big:** "10,000 hours of free evaluation" on the brand orange with the button, not the dashed form placeholder. The cookie-style notice is compact (the offer and the button only).

- **Fourth pass (27 Oct 2026, my own review after "keep iterating"):** Open data shows each Hugging Face repository as a card with a real frame from its main camera (episode 0, 3 s in; the G1 head camera is stereo, so its left half; `public/v8/data/*.jpg`), the robot, episodes and cameras. On narrow screens the section links open from a Menu button, the top-bar button shortens to "Apply", and the 10,000-hour notice becomes a one-line bar.

Shots are picked for robot motion and few or no people, so the face blur stays small. The product shots are not run through the blur: they show no faces and the pose model takes the rover for a person.

- **Hero line under the claim (user, 27 Oct 2026):** "Hundreds of Robots | Hundreds of Environments | Thousands of Evaluations" (the user wrote the first two; the third completes the climb and is true: ~15,900 recorded runs). The closing section is one sentence beside the button: "Bring your policy. We put it to the test on real robots, in the wild." Lab marks are fixed-size circles; Physical Intelligence's mark is a white π on a black disc.

- **The evaluations film is a trailer (user, 27 Oct 2026: "make it like a trailer... something I would say WOW"):** two title cards on black (*Every robot looks good / in a demo.*, *So we test them / in the wild.*), a montage that speeds up through nine real places, each named with its robot (under a tree, a campus walkway, a library, an office, a kitchen, a lobby, a park path, a canteen, by the window; YAM, SO-ARM101, Franka, G1, Earth Rover Mini+), one SO-ARM101 run held with *One instruction, written on the spot.*, then the pull-back to 6,561 runs with a counter racing up, and an end card *Thousands of evaluations. In the wild.* with the lockup. It opens on shots with nobody in frame; blurred faces only appear in sub-second montage cuts. The words are drawn into the film (Zalando Sans 800, Kode Mono for the robot tags), so the page no longer overlays a counter. `wild.mp4` (1600x900) and `wild-960.mp4` for phones, made by `tools/trailer.py` + `tools/trailer_shots.py` in the session scratchpad. No live figures in the film: they would go stale; the page carries those.

## What we are and how we say it (26 Oct 2026, BIT-3373)

The v5 copy got bad feedback ("copy is bad or misleading... messy... doesn't give strong flow or CTA"). The **launch film script** (with its interview guide) is the source of **facts and positioning**, not of copy: a first v6 draft that followed the film scene by scene and reused its lines was rejected the same day ("u took literally the interview and made it a website... dont COPY exactly what is mentioned in the interview"). Write the page in our own words, organised as a website, not as a film. The previous rules ("Evaluations made in the wild" as headline, the 10,000-hour *dataset* as call to action, "every number is live") are superseded.

- **Never lift film or interview lines verbatim** onto a page: not the tagline ("The proving ground for Physical AI"), the scene titles or the speakers' sentences. Use the ideas (no robot needed on your side, one protocol for everyone, every run published, free academic hours) in fresh wording.
- **Hierarchy (21 Oct 2026 huddle with Michael Cho, re-confirmed by the user on 26 Oct):** in the wild first, cross-embodiment second, scale third, partners as logos. The words **"in the wild" appear above the fold** (not only "real world"). Less text, more footage, especially in the hero. Benchmark detail (rankings, the method, a sample comparison) goes **below the fold**. Scale is shown on **several axes** (runs, scenes, instructions, policies, tasks, robot types; hours and locations once the API records them), never one metric. Earth Rover is always included.
- **Headline:** *Physical AI, in the wild.* (user pick, 26 Oct 2026, after "Benchmarking robot policies in the wild." was too long). Under it, one short line: humanoids, arms and rovers, running real models in homes, offices and campuses.
- **Blog (`/v7/blog/`):** Jiafei's draft post in his words (only the product-name typo in the title fixed), with the doc's two figures on white panels, the DCR equation in MathML and marked slots for the teaser and third-person videos still to come. It is linked from the top bar and the footer.
- **Plain section titles (user, 26 Oct 2026: "I hate that").** A section's title is its plain name: In the wild, Our robots, Our scale, The leaderboard, How it works, Evaluations, Open data, Academic access, FAQ. Never a clever line as the title ("Who leads on each robot.", "The record so far."); a descriptive line goes underneath as a short subtitle. Only the hero headline is a sentence.
- **Clear sections.** Every section after the hero is a full-width band with its own background (alternating `--paper` / `--band`, one light band for the academic offer). Its header is the plain title, the section link on the right of the same row (Full leaderboard, Browse all evaluations, Browse on Hugging Face), and the subtitle below.
- **Order:** hero (film) → founding partners strip (logos directly under the hero, user request of 22 Oct) → **In the wild** (the wall of twelve real evaluations, scenes per robot type) → **Robots** (the five robot types) → **Scale** (six live figures) → below the fold: Leaderboard → How it works (three steps, two kinds of score, one line each) → Evaluations (one real comparison) → Open data → Academic access → FAQ (includes fairness and "why real robots instead of simulation").
- **One call to action: "Submit your policy".** It appears in the top bar (the app pages get the same button when v7 becomes the site root; see commits 04f29e5 and d898f79 for that change), the hero, the end of How it works and the Academic access band. Every copy has the same label and the same destination. **No secondary buttons** (user rule, 26 Oct 2026): the leaderboard, evaluations, data and "Join as an evaluator" are plain links or footer items. Amber is used only for this button.
- **The offer (open question):** the launch film and the CoRL whitepaper describe *10,000 hours of free evaluation for academic researchers*; the 21 Oct huddle named *the 10,000-hour dataset* as the day-one call to action. The page currently says "Academic labs evaluate free, from a pool of 10,000 hours" under "Submit your policy". Until the user and Michael settle which it is, don't write either as the other.
- **Founding partners** (not "co-organizers"): NUS MAGIC Lab (evaluation protocol), BitRobot (robots and operations), EAIC Singapore (test sites in Singapore), Hugging Face (open data and LeRobot). The strip shows the logos only, with no role line under them (user request, 26 Oct 2026). **Always the official logos in their own colours**: never recolour, grey out or monochrome a partner logo (user correction, 22 Oct 2026).
- **Fleet size is never published** (user, 26 Oct 2026: there is no need to tell people, and the fleet keeps growing). Pages say **"hundreds of robots"**, never a count per robot type. The types: Unitree G1 humanoids, bimanual YAM stations, Franka arms (DROID setup), SO-ARM101, Earth Rovers (joining). Every other number is read from the API.
- **Robot cards and setups (v7, Jiafei's request):** the card photos are the team-supplied product shots (`public/v7/robots/`, shot on white, so the stages are light `#eceef1` with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`). A robot with a documented setup opens a dialog with its labelled setup photo (`public/v7/setups/`) and its parts list from the team's purchase sheet (`public/v7/setups.js`; shop links without tracking parameters; prices as listed, currency per supplier). Franka has no list in the sheet and links to the DROID hardware guide instead.
- **Locations (Jiafei, 26 Oct 2026), kept apart by track:** open-track evaluations run in Singapore, China, Seattle (United States) and Oxford (United Kingdom); fine-tuning datasets were recorded in Singapore, Kuala Lumpur (Malaysia) and China. No city in China, coordinates or per-site counts were given, so none are shown. Scale counts 5 countries.
- **Open data:** every evaluation run, failed attempts included, is released on Hugging Face in LeRobot format. The RoboColosseum org goes public at launch (user decision).
- **Words:** robot or robot type (not "body"), scene (not "room"), comparison and the evaluator's pick (not "verdict"), head-to-head (open track) and standard tasks (fine-tuning track) on public pages. Never "0–4 scale" (task maxima are 3–7), never "real rooms, not labs" (our partners are labs), no "independent" (BitRobot runs the fleet and is a partner), no "first" or "only" (RoboChallenge, PhAIL, RoboArena, XDOF and Artificial Analysis also evaluate on real robots).
- **No globe or map.** None of the competitors uses one and ours implied reach we don't have. Diversity is shown with real footage (the wall of twelve evaluations) and a live count of scenes.
- **Less text, more footage** still holds: the hero is the film, full bleed, with the claim over it.
- **Voice:** plain, specific, sentence case, from the researcher's side ("you", "your policy").

## Type

## Leaderboards (22 Oct 2026)

- Modelled on Artificial Analysis (summary cards naming the leaders with lab logos, dense table with logo cells, filters, key definitions) and Arena (dark rows with numbered rank chips, logo tiles, score bars inside the rows, category tabs with icons).
- Page order: header (body · track) → body tabs with the robot glyphs → track switch with icons → four summary cards (leader with lab tile, comparisons or trials, policies, lead over #2 or median progress) → the ranking → the Robocurve figure → key definitions.
- Ranking rows: rank chip (#1 amber, #2–3 raised), lab tile + policy + lab + Hugging Face link, a bar per metric (open: rating with ±1 SE whisker on a shared axis; fine-tuning: full success fill with the Wilson 95% band, progress fill), W·T·L stacked record, counts in mono, a play icon to the evaluations. Fewer than 20 comparisons (or 10 trials) is faded and marked low evidence; pending policies are hidden behind a toggle.
- Sorting on the metric columns, text filter by policy or lab, minimum-comparisons chips (open) or task chips (fine-tuning: "All tasks" pools a checkpoint across tasks).
- Lab logos come from `src/labs.js` (same mapping as `public/landing/leaderboard.js`); icons are the inline set in `src/components/Icon.vue`.
