# RoboColosseum design language

The decisions behind the site (landing page, blog and app pages), as the user stated them. Read this before touching any page. When a decision changes, change it here first. This file describes the site as it is; how it got here is in git history.

## One site, no versions (30 Oct 2026)

- **There is one site and it is changed in place** (user, 30 Oct 2026: "stop versioning /v8/ or /v9/ etc. Remove all of that, let's keep only the latest version... dont use even internally. We have git history"). Never create versioned folders, files or names (`/v10/`, `v9.css`, "the v9 page" in comments or docs). To try a direction, use a branch and its Netlify deploy preview. Earlier drafts exist only in git history; replaced films and raw footage are archived outside git in `RoboColosseum/media/`.
- **Where things live:**
  - `public/landing/`: the landing page, served at the site root (`netlify.toml` rewrites `/` to `/landing/index.html`; the page resolves its assets from the site root, so it also works under GitHub Pages' `/robo-colosseum/`). `index.html`, `landing.css` (also the blog's base stylesheet), `landing.js`, `chrome.js` (top bar links, lockups, the call to action, theme switch, footer), `live.js` (API data), `board.js`, `leaderboard.js` (lab marks and the Robocurve figure, also imported by the app's leaderboard), `viewer.js`, `datasets.js`, `setups.js`; media in `video/`, `robots/`, `setups/`, `datasets/` (repository frames) and `assets/` (partner and lab logos).
  - `public/blog/`: the blog index and one folder per post.
  - `public/brand/`: the logo files.
  - `src/`: the app (leaderboard, evaluations, tasks, submit, join).
  - Addresses of the old drafts (`/v3/` to `/v9/`, `/pink/`, `/orange/`) redirect to the root, and `/v7/blog/` to the blog post.
- **Top menu** (user, 30 Oct 2026: "adjust the top menu so its not very bloated of options"): **Leaderboard, Evaluations, Embodiments, How it works, Blog**, the same on the landing page, the blog and the app. On the landing page the first four scroll to its sections; elsewhere Leaderboard and Evaluations open the app pages, and Embodiments and How it works open the landing's sections. Open data and FAQ live in the footer (Open data goes to Hugging Face; FAQ sits under "Take part"). On narrow screens the links open from a Menu button.

## Track names and which tasks exist (standing rules, 28 Oct 2026)

- **The two tracks are always "Open track" and "Fine-tune track"** (user, 28 Oct 2026: "lets standardize on the term"). This applies to every public page, the blog and the app: tabs, headings, labels, notes, FAQ, alt and aria text. Capitalise the first word, lower-case "track". Never "Head-to-head", "Standard tasks", "fine-tuning track", "Fine-tuning" or "kind of score" for a track. Describing what happens inside a track stays plain: Open track **comparisons** (blind, two models, the evaluator's pick) and Fine-tune track **trials** (25 attempts per model and task). API values (`track=fine-tuning`, `'fine'`) are identifiers, not copy, and do not change.
- **Datasets are "Fine-tune track datasets"**, never "training sets": the demonstrations a model is fine-tuned on for one task. Evaluation runs on Hugging Face stay "evaluation sets".
- **A task exists while it is on the published Fine-tune track leaderboard.** The router's `eval_tasks` table is where tasks live, and the hourly snapshot (`public_leaderboard_data`, `/api/leaderboard?track=fine-tuning`) lists the ones with results. Pages never list a task or its dataset on their own say:
  - `public/landing/datasets.js` ties every Fine-tune track dataset to its router task id, and `liveSets()` shows only those whose task is on the published board (landing Open data and the app's Tasks page). A task removed from the router leaves the page at the next hourly refresh, with no frontend change.
  - When a task is retired, move its dataset to `RETIRED` in that file with the date and the reason, and add a line under "Retired tasks" here.
  - The app's Tasks page lists the real tasks from the published board (no draft or example cards).
- **Retired tasks:**
  - Franka **pour water** (`droid-pour-water`, 100 episodes): not one of the five Franka tasks since the 26 Oct 2026 Franka correction (close laptop, open drawer, mug on coaster, put marker pen, towel in basket); the router has no task for it. Removed from the site on 28 Oct 2026. The Hugging Face repository is kept.
  - The five draft Franka task cards in the app (carrot in bowl, pour corn, flip pot, microwave door, cup in drawer) were examples that never became tasks; removed on 28 Oct 2026.

## What we are and how we say it

Sources: the 21 Oct 2026 huddle with Michael Cho, the launch film script and interview guide (facts and positioning only, never copy), and the 26 Oct 2026 launch meeting (launch on 4 Nov: X thread, website and launch video together).

- **Hierarchy:** in the wild first, cross-embodiment second, scale third, the founders as logos. The words **"in the wild" appear above the fold**. Less text, more footage, especially in the hero. Benchmark detail (rankings, the method, a sample comparison) goes **below the fold**. Scale is shown on **several axes**, never one metric. Earth Rover is always included.
- **Hero:** it must say evaluation **and** benchmark (user, 27 Oct 2026: "It should have both evaluation AND benchmark"). *Benchmarking Physical AI / Eval In the Wild*, on two lines over the film, then "Hundreds of Robots | Hundreds of Environments | Thousands of Evaluations" and the button. No status pills or stat lines in the hero ("those top notes 15k runs... I don't like them").
- **One call to action: "Apply for free evaluation"** (an expression of interest; "Submit your policy" asked too much, since onboarding takes real engineering work). **Every copy opens the Youform https://app.youform.com/forms/riubycrq directly, in a new tab; the form is never embedded in the page** (user, 28 Oct 2026: an embed was "far from what i want"). The address is `FORM_URL` in `public/landing/chrome.js` (landing page and blog) and `APPLY` in `src/cta.js` (app). Every copy has the same label and destination. **No secondary buttons**: the leaderboard, evaluations, data and "Join as an evaluator" are outlined section buttons, plain links or footer items. Orange is used only for this button, the offer and a picked run. Follow-up: Ian contacts applicants; strong university fits go to Jiafei.
- **The offer is 10,000 hours of free evaluation.** It follows the reader as a compact cookie-style notice (the offer and the button, bottom right, a one-line bar on phones; it appears once the reader scrolls, steps aside while the closing section is on screen and stays closed for the visit once dismissed), and the page closes on it, big, on the brand orange: "10,000 hours of free evaluation" with "Bring your policy. We put it to the test on real robots, in the wild." and the button. Eligibility is not stated beyond the FAQ ("teams with a policy for one of our robots"); allocation is the team's call.
- **Order of the landing page:** hero (film, claim, button) → "Founded by" strip → The leaderboard → Real-world evaluations → Multiple embodiments → Our scale → How it works (four steps, the two tracks) → Evaluation viewer → Open data → FAQ (including fairness and "why real robots instead of simulation") → the 10,000-hour close.
- **Plain section titles** (user, 26 Oct 2026: "I hate that"). A section's title is its plain name: The leaderboard, Real-world evaluations, Multiple embodiments, Our scale, How it works, Evaluation viewer, Open data, FAQ. Never a clever line as the title; a descriptive line goes underneath as a short subtitle. Only the hero headline is a sentence.
- **Clear sections:** every section after the hero is a full-width band, alternating paper and white. Its header is the plain title, the section's outlined button with an arrow on the right of the same row ("Full leaderboard", "Open the evaluation viewer"; Open data carries "Get 10,000 hours of free evals" with the Hugging Face logo, which opens the form like every call to action), and the subtitle below.
- **Numbers are counted hourly, not live**, and carry the date ("Updated 28 Oct 2026") on the leaderboard and the scale band. They come from `/api/stats` and the published boards, written by the hourly `robocolosseum-leaderboard` job. If `/api/stats` is unavailable the page counts the same figures from the evaluation list in the browser (slower, fallback only).
- **Leaderboard preview:** the top five per robot and track; when a robot has more, "Show all N policies" under the list reveals every row and "Show top 5" folds them away. The choice holds while the reader switches robots. It is a plain text button with a chevron, not a call to action.
- **Our scale:** one large total (recorded runs) with a bar splitting it into Open track runs and Fine-tune track trials, then four cards that show breadth: scenes as one square per scene by robot, the evaluators' own instructions scrolling, the logos of the labs whose policies were evaluated, and the countries and embodiments.
- **Fleet size is never published** (user, 26 Oct 2026: the fleet keeps growing). Pages say **"hundreds of robots"**, never a count per robot type. The types: Unitree G1 humanoids, bimanual YAM stations, Franka arms (DROID setup), SO-ARM101, Earth Rover Mini+ (joining). Every other number is read from the API.
- **Locations (Jiafei, 26 Oct 2026), kept apart by track:** Open track evaluations run in Singapore, China, Seattle (United States) and Oxford (United Kingdom); Fine-tune track datasets were recorded in Singapore, Kuala Lumpur (Malaysia) and China. No city in China, coordinates or per-site counts were given, so none are shown. Scale counts 5 countries.
- **Open data:** every evaluation run, failed attempts included, is released on Hugging Face in LeRobot format; the page lists the actual repositories as cards with a real frame from the main camera (episode 0, 3 s in; the G1 head camera is stereo, so its left half; `public/landing/datasets/*.jpg`), the robot, episodes and cameras. The RoboColosseum org goes public at launch (user decision). The Earth Rover drives stay off the page until their release, licence and GPS precision are decided.
- **Never lift film or interview lines verbatim** onto a page: not the tagline ("The proving ground for Physical AI"), the scene titles or the speakers' sentences. Use the ideas in fresh wording, organised as a website, not as a film.
- **Words:** robot, robot type or embodiment (not "body"), scene (not "room"), comparison and the evaluator's pick (not "verdict"), Open track and Fine-tune track. Never "0–4 scale" (task maxima are 3–7), never "real rooms, not labs" (our partners are labs), no "independent" (BitRobot runs the fleet and is a partner), no "first" or "only" (RoboChallenge, PhAIL, RoboArena, XDOF and Artificial Analysis also evaluate on real robots).
- **No globe or map.** Diversity is shown with real footage and live counts.
- **Voice:** plain, specific, sentence case, from the researcher's side ("you", "your policy").

## Founders, robots and setups

- **Founding partners** (not "co-organizers"): NUS MAGIC Lab, BitRobot, EAIC Singapore, Hugging Face. The "Founded by" strip under the hero shows the logos only, in one row with equal gaps, each sized by eye to the same visual weight; only the logo itself is a link (hover dims it); a 2×2 grid on phones. **In the light theme the strip is grey** (29 Oct 2026, user: "i only asked the founded by companies logos"): `filter: grayscale(1)`, each logo's own colours back on hover; the dark theme keeps the colours. **Every other logo stays in its own colours** (AI labs' marks, the Hugging Face logo). Never redraw or retint a logo file; the grey is a filter only. EAIC uses its official letters (`public/landing/assets/eaic-mark.webp`, red and black, transparent); its black "i" vanishes on dark paper, so the dark theme puts it on a light `#f1f1f1` plate.
- **Robot cards:** cut-outs of the team's product photos on the peach stage `#f2e3d9` (the dark theme's stage is `#cbcbcb`), with no blend mode, so white robots stay white (`public/landing/robots/`). They are cut from the full-size photos in `v7-updates/landing_page_pics/` with rembg's `birefnet-general`, which opens the gaps a robot encloses (cable loops, gripper jaws, between links); dark edge pixels have the white backdrop unmixed, white plastic keeps its own rim. Check a new cut-out on a saturated colour. **The G1 photo carries no retailer mark** ("ROBOSTORE" was retouched off the chest; the Unitree triangle is the maker's mark and stays). The app's robot tabs use the same files and stage.
- **Setups:** a robot with a documented setup opens a dialog with its labelled setup photo (`public/landing/setups/`) and its parts list from the team's purchase sheet (`public/landing/setups.js`; shop links without tracking parameters; prices as listed, currency per supplier). Franka has no list in the sheet and links to the DROID hardware guide.

## Footage

- **Every face in every video is blurred** (user, 27 Oct 2026: "BLUR all people's faces. This is a general direction for ALL videos from now on"). People are found with a pose model (YOLO11m-pose) at full resolution before any downscaling, and a padded head region is blurred and held for a few frames; humanoid-only and product shots are left alone (the model takes humanoids and rovers for people). Live recordings streamed by the evaluation viewer are not processed.
- **Hero film:** the agency's BITROBOT-39.2 (20 s, 9 shots), full bleed behind the claim and dark in both themes, with a shade from the lower left. `public/landing/video/hero.mp4`, `hero-720.mp4` for phones, `hero.jpg`, and `hero.json`, whose captions name the **embodiment and location only, no dates** (user request). Every shot was checked for faces, so the agency's export is used as delivered. Opening on the room full of G1 humanoids, never on a blurry shot ("starting with a very high blurry video really sucks").
- **Real-world evaluations trailer** (user, 27 Oct 2026: "make it like a trailer... something I would say WOW"): two title cards (*Every robot looks good / in a demo.*, *So we test them / in the wild.*), a montage that speeds up through nine named places, one SO-ARM101 run held with *One instruction, written on the spot.*, the pull-back to 6,561 runs with a counter, and the end card *Thousands of evaluations. In the wild.* The words are drawn into the film, which carries no live figures (they would go stale). `public/landing/video/wild.mp4` (1600×900) and `wild-960.mp4` for phones, with `wild.jpg` as the poster.
- Videos play only while visible, and never for readers who ask for reduced motion.

## Blog (30 Oct 2026)

- **`/blog/`** lists the posts, newest first; each post lives at `/blog/<slug>/` (`public/blog/`). Both use the landing page's top bar, footer and stylesheet (`landing.css`) plus `public/blog/blog.css` and `blog.js`. A post has a contents list beside the text on wide screens, figures wider than the reading column, diagrams on white panels in both themes (they are drawn on white), equations in MathML, and the site's own blurred videos.
- **A post keeps its author's words.** The page applies only the site's rules: the track names, the one call to action (the post ends on the 10,000-hour close with its button, and a line about taking part points to the application form, not to a repository), and plain typo fixes. Every change to an author's text is listed for the author to approve before the copy is final.
- **First post:** Jiafei's *RoboColosseum: A Scientific Megaproject for Evaluating Physical Intelligence In-the-Wild*, from the Google Doc `1Tpvcdv_c_vLcNn8NcwB-nwu2PiJmu303ktbFHcaadEI` (it exports without login: `/export?format=html` carries the images). The logo's dark wordmark stays silver.

- Tokens live in `public/landing/landing.css` (landing and blog) and `src/styles/arena.css` (app, under the app's own token names).

## Type

- Zalando Sans for every word (SemiBold with tight tracking for headings, as the brand slides' −5%); Kode Mono Bold in capitals for labels, stamps and data, each live value marked with a small orange square. Both from Google Fonts.
- Zalando Sans's tabular figures draw a slashed zero and widen the space character, so big figures use its default digits and cards holding words never use `tabular-nums`.

## Data and loading

- Everything is read from `/api` (proxied by Netlify to the router on Render) through `public/landing/live.js`. The landing page's data requests are the two leaderboard calls, `/api/stats` and the viewer's video lookups; it never fetches `/api/list_ab_evaluations` unless `/api/stats` fails.
- The evaluation viewer looks several recent comparisons up at once and shows the first with both runs recorded, since recordings reach storage hours after a comparison is judged.

## Leaderboards (22 Oct 2026)

- **28 Oct 2026:** public leaderboard labels show model names without checkpoint/version suffixes such as `rig-v1` or commit hashes. Keep the full identities in data and evaluation links. The Open track shows the backend's active deployment/checkpoint selection; older EE LLM results remain in evaluation history.
- Modelled on Artificial Analysis (summary cards naming the leaders with lab logos, dense table with logo cells, filters, key definitions) and Arena (rows with numbered rank chips, logo tiles, score bars inside the rows, category tabs with icons).
- Page order: header (robot · track) → robot tabs with photos → track switch → four summary cards (leader with lab tile, comparisons or trials, policies, lead over #2 or median progress) → the ranking → the Robocurve figure → key definitions.
- Ranking rows: rank chip, lab tile + policy + lab + Hugging Face link, a bar per metric (Open track: rating with ±1 SE whisker on a shared axis; Fine-tune track: full success with the Wilson 95% band, and progress), W·T·L record, counts in mono, a play icon to the evaluations. Fewer than 20 comparisons (or 10 trials) is faded and marked low evidence; pending policies are hidden behind a toggle.
- Sorting on the metric columns, text filter by policy or lab, minimum-comparisons chips (Open track) or task chips (Fine-tune track: "All tasks" pools a checkpoint across tasks).
- Lab logos come from `src/labs.js` (same mapping as `public/landing/leaderboard.js`); icons are the inline set in `src/components/Icon.vue`.

## App pages

- **The app wears the landing page's brand** (user, 27–28 Oct 2026: "all sub-pages need to follow the main website visuals"):
  - **Top bar and footer:** the landing's, with the same menu (see "Top menu" above), the shared theme switch and the one orange button, which opens the Youform in a new tab (`src/cta.js`).
  - **Tokens:** the landing's colours under the app's token names in `src/styles/arena.css`: paper `#f1f1f1`, white panels and boards (`--panel`/`--paper-2` `#fff`, hover `--raised` `#f6f6f6`), orange `#ff6200`, stage `#f2e3d9`; the dark theme as on the landing, with the grain only there.
  - **Data marks:** ink, like the landing's leaderboard. The win/tie/loss bar runs from ink to hairline. Orange marks only the button and a picked run.
  - **Words:** the landing's: Open track and Fine-tune track, robot (not body), scene (not room), pick (not verdict). The Fine-tune track score is success and progress, never "0–4" or "4 of 4".
  - **Pages:** Submit is the free-evaluation page, with the landing's four steps and the same button. Join keeps its own form button outlined (its form id is still a placeholder). The app root sends visitors to the landing page.
- Every app page opens the same way: mono eyebrow → h1 "Robot · Track" → one-sentence lede → a live tag on the right; then the robot tabs and the track switch (`src/components/BoardControls.vue`); then four summary cards; then one `.board` panel with a toolbar (search, chips, count, refresh) and the content; then definitions as `<details>`. Shared rules live in `src/styles/boards.css`; page-specific rules stay scoped.
- Evaluations, Open track: a two-column feed + review. Feed items carry time-ago, a pick chip (orange when a side was preferred, plain for ties), the instruction and "A vs B · scene". The review card quotes the instruction, shows both sides with progress bars (the preferred side outlined, the pick stamped between), the evaluator / scene / difficulty (label · n/10) row (the API returns an empty evaluator when no name was provided; the UI falls back to the institution, then an em dash) and the evaluator's note; below it the two camera packages. The feed lists only comparisons with at least one video source on each side; signed playback URLs are fetched when a comparison is selected.
- Evaluations, Fine-tune track: the same feed and card for scored trials (outcome chips, score with a bar, episode, criteria, note, cameras) and for imported datasets (episode select, the synchronized player with play/pause, scrubber and speed).
- Tasks: the Open track explains how an instruction is written on the spot; the Fine-tune track lists the real tasks per robot from the published board, with results by model and the dataset link.
- Numbers: `fmt` and `pct` from `src/labs.js`; time-ago in mono; never a bare percentage in a sentence.
